Findings

The six things we check on every visit, and what we found on this one.
  • 1. The address exists and matches the listing Confirmed

    C/ Fuencarral 45 is a residential building with a portal on the street, not an office or a shop. The flat shown to us was 4.º izq., the same one described in the listing.

  • 2. The rooms match the photos you were sent Confirmed, with a note

    The living room, kitchen, bathroom and main bedroom match the listing photos, including the furniture. The second bedroom does not: in the listing it has a window onto the street, and in the flat it faces the interior courtyard and is noticeably darker and smaller. The landlord said that photo was taken in another unit in the same building. Ask him to send you a photo of the actual room before you commit.

  • 3. Someone was there to show the apartment Confirmed

    He was at the portal within a few minutes of the agreed time, had keys to the flat, and gave a full unhurried walkthrough with no rooms kept shut.

  • 4. The landlord's name matches what he told you Confirmed

    He showed a Spanish ID card. The name on it is the same one used on the listing and in your WhatsApp messages. We only checked that the names match — we did not copy, photograph or keep the document number.

  • 5. No full payment was demanded before a viewing Confirmed

    He asks for one month's deposit plus the first month, paid when the contract is signed, and said he is happy to sign in person once you arrive. Nothing was requested to reserve or hold the flat in the meantime.

  • 6. Red flags in the terms you were offered One thing to clarify

    The advertised €1,150 does not include the community fee, which he put at about €45 a month, or heating in winter. That was not stated in the listing. It is a normal arrangement in Madrid, but get the full monthly figure written into the contract so there is no argument later.

What this report does not tell you

So you know exactly where our check stops.
  • Hidden defects that weren't visible on the day — behind furniture, under flooring, or inside walls.
  • Noise or anything else at times other than when we visited.
  • The legal validity of the contract he offers. We are not lawyers and we do not review clauses.
  • Anything that changes after the visit — a different flat being offered, a new price, or a new payment method.

What we would do next, in your position

  1. Ask for a photo of the real second bedroom before you send anything, and keep it in the same chat as the rest of the conversation.
  2. Get the total monthly figure in writing — rent, community fee and bills — and check it against the €1,150 you were quoted.
  3. Pay by bank transfer to an account in his name, once the contract is signed. Not to a third party, and not by an instant transfer app.
